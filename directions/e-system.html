4 · The typography

Inter, at your request

Inter, everywhere, as you asked that afternoon. One family in three weights: 400 for anything read, 500 for headings, product names and the statements, 600 only for the very biggest line. Uppercase and tracked at twelve per cent for the nav, the tags and the buttons. Tabular figures so prices sit in a column. It is the family the reference runs on, which is why the two will feel related; what keeps ours ours is the wordmark, the flower and the photography, none of which are type. The serif proposed that morning is retired.

Inter 500 and 600 · headings, product names, statements

Bô — three pieces, all black.

Cocody is a district of Abidjan. We only put the name on something once it deserves to go back there.

Signature Zip-Up Sweater · Wide-Leg Pleated Trouser · Cocody Monogram Hat

Inter 400 and 500 · nav, labels, prices, forms, the marquee, the legal line

Body text is Inter 400, in sentence case, at sixteen pixels: the story on a product page, the about page, the shipping notes. Uppercase and tracked at twelve per cent is reserved for the nav, the tags and the buttons, so it keeps its edge.

What takes the medium weight

Every heading, every product name, the statements, the footer's invitation, the pop-up titles. Tracked a little tighter than Inter's default, because at display size it sits loose. The italic is the emphasis.

What stays regular

Everything the reader operates or reads at length: the nav, prices, sizes, buttons, forms, the bag, the marquee, the legal line, the stories. Tabular figures throughout, so prices sit in a column.

The two rules

The wordmark is never set in type. It is the studio's vector on every page, and nothing imitates it.

The signature stays the handwritten artwork. It is the one mark still a PNG, and the one still worth asking the studio for.

2 · The masthead

The wordmark, then the flower

The wordmark sits where NOLOGO sits: in the middle of the bar, on every page — a quarter smaller since 7 October, as you asked, with the menu, search and bag beside it as thin line icons, as on the screenshot you sent. The moment the page is moving — past the film on the homepage, past the first hundred pixels elsewhere — it gives way to the flower, and the flower turns with the scroll — since 7 October with its hole, as on the logo, cut out so the bar shows through it. It stops when you stop. A mark that never stops turning is what “gimmicky” looks like, and the brief asked for premium.

This one turns with the page too. Scroll and watch it.

On the homepage

The bar is transparent over the film, with the wordmark in white. Open a menu and the gradient comes down white with the type in black, as on the reference, for as long as it is open. Once the film is gone it is the same solid bar every other page has, with the flower in it.

Everywhere else

The bar is solid from the start. Scroll a shop page and the swap happens after the first screen. Scroll back to the top and the wordmark returns.

If you would rather it spun on its own

One line changes it to a slow, constant turn. We would not: the scroll-linked version is the one that feels like the site is responding to you.

1 · The marquee

The voucher, and the shipping

The strip at the top of every page: ten per cent off the first order for joining, free shipping over the threshold, and “Shipped worldwide” — since 7 October, nothing more. It moves at a walking pace, pauses when you hover it, and can be closed. The separator is the flower. Since 23 September it is thinner, as you asked: 26 pixels tall against 31.

The voucher

Clicking it goes to the signup at the foot of the page. In WooCommerce it is a coupon restricted to first orders and sent by the welcome mail — a setting, not a plugin.

The free-shipping line

€100 is a placeholder, sized against a catalogue priced €20 to €50. It is decision 6 in the document, and it has to be settled before checkout is configured.

Why it is not on the checkout page

Nothing should move on the page where someone is paying. The checkout keeps the bar and drops the strip.

6 · The footer

All the way down

On every page, and since 7 October built on the Nude Project footer you sent: the signup the voucher points at, in one row with the three groups and Help; the social accounts beside a country and language selector; the legal pages; the colophon, with the flower in place of the stacked wordmark; and the wordmark on one line across the full width at the very foot. Scroll to the bottom of any page and the page slides up off it — the footer stays where it is, underneath. That happens wherever the whole footer fits the screen, which is a laptop and most phones; on a screen too short for it the footer simply follows the page, because one held still could never be read to its top. On a phone the four link columns fold. Nude Project’s store locations have no counterpart yet: when there is a shop, it goes in the Maison Cocody column.

In the grid a sold-out piece fades — the photograph and the words together — and says so in brackets under its name, above the price, as the reference does; it stays a link to its page and comes back a little under the pointer. The chip on the picture is kept only for a piece with a few sizes left, in the ochre. 8 · The account

One page, three forms

Your note of 30 September: a login page, on this direction. There is a person in the bar now, between search and the bag, on every page. It opens one page with one narrow column in the middle of it, in the same type, the same square fields and the same black button as the rest of the shop. Sign in is what you land on; creating an account and resetting a password take its place in the same column rather than being pages of their own, so there is one address to remember and one link to find. The reference passes its customers to Shopify’s own account page for this; ours stays on the site.

Where it is

The person icon on the right of the bar. On a narrow phone a second icon beside the bag would push the wordmark off centre, so there it is in the menu, with Search and the bag. Your account is also in the footer, under Help.

What it holds

Sign in: email, password, keep me signed in, forgot your password. Create an account: an email and a password, nothing more, and an unticked box for the 10% and the collection mails. Reset your password: one field. Each answers with one line once it is sent.

Three questions back

Nothing is checked, stored or sent yet; it is the design. Should an account stay optional, so a first order can still be paid for as a guest? We would keep it that way. Is the 10% for joining the list, for making an account, or either? And the pages behind the login — orders, addresses, details — are WooCommerce’s own and are not drawn yet: say if you want them.

9 · The review of 7 October

The rest of your list

The points that do not belong to the bar, the strip, the flower or the footer, which are in their own sections above.

Whole pictures

The pieces were cut off because the photographs are not all square — the hats and a tote are wide, the trouser is tall — and every frame cropped them to fill it. Now a product photograph sits whole in its frame, on the same white as the seamless: in the shop, on the homepage, in the related pieces and on the product page. The trouser shows it best.

A more fluid product page

No more one picture and a row of thumbnails. On a laptop the pictures are a column you scroll — the first full width, then in pairs — while the name, the sizes and the button hold still beside them; on a phone they are a strip you swipe, one at a time, with the count under it. The sweater has the most pictures.

The size finder

It worked from the body-mass index alone, so a tall, heavy person came out as an L. Now height and weight are each read against what a size is cut for, the bigger answer wins, and past the top of XL it says that XL will be tight or short and points at the chart instead. The ranges are ours, set against the chart; yours, from the fit model, would replace them. The flower around the chosen size is a sixth smaller.

The basket

The empty bag says so in words, without the big flower.

Your posts on the site: yes

It is possible, and the homepage now has the band for it, Worn by you, with the shoot’s own pictures standing in. Instagram lets a business account read the public posts that tag it and the ones under a hashtag, and a WordPress plugin or a service such as Flowbox or EmbedSocial pulls them in. Two conditions: each post is approved before it shows, and its owner is asked before it is used on a shop. TikTok is patchier, and depends on the service.

The phone

Everything above is on the phone too, checked at 390 and 360 pixels wide. The one place the type differed from the laptop was the phone menu, set large and in sentence case; it is now the side panel, in the same small capitals as the laptop’s bar. If you meant something else by the font, a screenshot from the phone would settle it.

Still yours to decide

Thirteen things
  • The copyright year. 2024 is in, as you wrote it; confirm it is the year the site started.
  • Opposites Attract. You wrote “Opposite Attracts”; the shop and the jerseys say “Opposites Attract”, so that is what the Archive says. Tell us if the drop is being renamed.
  • The categories. Tops, Bottoms, Headwear and Accessories are our grouping of the catalogue. Correct it on the call.
  • Contact & press. The part of the About page the menu points at has three slots — orders, press, stockists — and needs the addresses.
  • The fit ranges. The size finder works from heights and weights we set against the chart. The ones your fit model and your customers actually wear would make it right.
  • Your posts on the site. It needs the Instagram account to be a business account, a tool to pull the posts in, and someone to approve them. Say if you want it, and which handle and hashtag.
  • Where the back-in-stock addresses go. The form is on every sold-out page and thanks the person, but nothing receives the address yet: WooCommerce’s own notices, or the mailing list the maison uses?
  • The account. The login page is drawn and nothing sits behind it yet. Does an account stay optional, with guest checkout kept? Is the 10% for joining the list or for making an account? And do you want the pages behind the login — orders, addresses, details — drawn too?
  • The voucher mechanics. Ten per cent on the first order only, or on the first order after signing up? One use per email? It changes the WooCommerce coupon and the welcome mail.
  • The social handles, the legal texts, the company numbers. The footer has the slots; it needs the words.
  • The free-shipping threshold, and who pays duties outside the EU. Decision 6, unchanged.
  • The shop you liked. The third screenshot of 21 September did not come through — only the NOLOGO phone screen and the Inter specimen arrived. Send it again and it goes in.
